# Adisha Madhushanka — WordPress and headless CMS

WordPress development, website management and headless CMS architecture.

## WordPress experience

Adisha's WordPress experience covers both development and website management, and it sits alongside his React, Next.js and Node.js work.

His WordPress experience began with his first role, at WAD International (July 2015 – July 2016), where he developed and customised WordPress websites for corporate and hospitality clients. WordPress was also part of the team's stack at MindLogics Limited (January 2026 – August 2026). The rest of his WordPress experience isn't tied to a particular employer.

## What he builds with WordPress

Adisha develops custom WordPress themes and plugins. He builds pages with Gutenberg blocks, Advanced Custom Fields (ACF) and common page builders.

He turns Figma or other design files into responsive, accessible and fast pages. His approach to accessibility is the same as in his other frontend work: semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, focus behaviour, colour contrast and accessible labels.

## Website management

Adisha manages WordPress websites day to day. This covers WordPress core, theme and plugin updates, security hardening, backups, hosting, site migrations and troubleshooting.

## Performance and SEO

Adisha works on site performance against Core Web Vitals, and on technical and on-page SEO.

## Headless WordPress

Adisha uses WordPress as a headless CMS, serving content through the WordPress REST API and GraphQL to front ends built with React and Next.js.

He designs content models so that marketing teams can edit content without help from a developer.

He chooses between a traditional WordPress site and a headless one based on the project's performance needs, how the content will be edited, and the budget.

## Typical problems and how he approaches them

These are the kinds of problems this work involves, described in general terms rather than as specific client projects.

Keeping a site current without breaking it: core, theme and plugin updates are part of looking after a site, together with backups, security hardening and troubleshooting when something goes wrong.

Pages that load slowly: he works on Core Web Vitals and technical SEO, so pages are fast for visitors and easier for search engines to read.

Marketing teams waiting on developers for content changes: he designs content models, custom blocks and ACF fields so the people who own the content can change it themselves.

Picking the right architecture: rather than defaulting to headless or traditional WordPress, he weighs the project's performance, editing and budget needs.

Moving a site: he handles hosting changes and site migrations.

## Value to the business

For a business, this means a website the marketing team can update without developer help, pages built from the design that are responsive, accessible and fast, and a site that is kept updated, secured and backed up.

Because Adisha is also a full-stack JavaScript engineer, he can take WordPress further than themes and plugins: headless front ends in React and Next.js, integrations with other systems through APIs, and AI features such as the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) chat on this site.
